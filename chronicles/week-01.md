# The Chronicle of Week 1 — The First Battles of Olympus

Long before the final banner was raised upon Mount Olympus, there was Week 1.

No army yet knew the part it would play in the story.

Some who looked mighty would later stumble. Others would discover strength they did not yet know they possessed. Victories that seemed decisive would fade in importance, while narrow escapes and painful defeats would eventually take on meanings no one could have understood at the time.

The army that would one day conquer Mount Olympus had already taken the field.

No one yet knew which banner it carried.

The Battle for Mount Olympus began with six wars.

## Hercules Unchained vs. Aries It Out
### The Awakening of Hercules
**Hercules Unchained 139.30 • Aries It Out 83.70**

Aries It Out struck first. By the end of Thursday, Aries held a commanding **31.4–0 lead**, appearing to seize the first stones of victory while Hercules Unchained had yet to answer.

Then Sunday came. Trevor Lawrence struck for **26.1 points**. Ashton Jeanty added **29.7**. Jahmyr Gibbs erupted for **31.1**, carving his name into the **Hall of Living Legends** before the first week of the Odyssey had even ended.

A 31-point deficit became an **89.7–59.6 advantage**. Hercules finished with **139.30 points**, the highest score produced by any army in Week 1. The final margin, **55.60 points**, became the greatest slaughter of the opening campaign.

And with it, **Atlas' Mountains changed hands**. Hercules Unchained emerged as the first **High King** of the season and posted a score that would have defeated every other army in Olympus.

Aries had struck first. History remembered that. It also remembered how completely Hercules answered.

## The Peasants of Troy vs. Winging It
### The Siege That Would Not End
**The Peasants of Troy 110.12 • Winging It 106.36**

The Peasants of Troy opened their campaign exactly as peasants fighting gods probably should have. They built a lead and then desperately tried not to lose it.

By the end of Thursday, Troy stood ahead **39.7–13.1**. Sunday tested every stone. Bijan Robinson delivered **27.3 points**. Nico Collins added 17.7. Juwan Johnson supplied another 12.9.

But Winging It had summoned a champion of its own. **D'Andre Swift erupted for 31.9 points**, earning a place in the **Hall of Living Legends**. Pittsburgh's defense struck for another 20, and what had once looked like a comfortable Troy victory began collapsing inward.

By the final horn, only **3.76 points** remained. **110.12–106.36.** The Peasants survived, and **Hector's Ramparts belonged to Troy**.

The Peasants produced only the ninth-highest score among the twelve armies that week. Eight teams had scored more. None of that mattered. The opponent standing before them had scored less.

The first **Highway Robbery** of the Olympian campaign belonged to a band of peasants who had somehow wandered into a war of gods and immediately stolen a province.

For Winging It, the defeat carried a different sting. There were **74.5 points sitting unused on its bench**, an entire sleeping host that might have changed the battle completely.

## TroyisBetterthanOdyssey vs. 🏺Hades Nutz
### When the Gates of Hades Opened
**🏺Hades Nutz 137.26 • TroyisBetterthanOdyssey 87.70**

For two days, TroyisBetterthanOdyssey held the advantage. After Thursday the scoreboard still favored Troy: **17.1–16.0.** It was only one point, but it was enough to believe.

Then Josh Allen arrived. Allen unleashed **35.7 points**, one of the greatest performances of the opening week, and entered the **Hall of Living Legends**. David Montgomery struck for 27.4. Parker Washington added 16.8. Bucky Irving delivered another 16.8.

In only a few hours, the underworld had swallowed the battlefield. Hades Nutz produced roughly **117 points during Sunday's early games alone**, transforming what had been a one-point deficit into a rout.

The final score was **137.26–87.70**. **Winged Messenger's Rest fell to Hades Nutz**, and the victors finished Week 1 with the second-highest score in all of Olympus.

## Winged Victory Formation vs. The Beer of Olympus
### Six-Tenths From Immortality
**Winged Victory Formation 118.16 • The Beer of Olympus 117.56**

Winged Victory Formation seemed to have already won it. Lamar Jackson delivered **25.0 points**. Justin Jefferson struck for **27.2**. DJ Moore added 18.5. By the end of Sunday's early games, Winged Victory led **57.8–20.4**.

Then Monday arrived, and Kenneth Walker began to run. Walker erupted for **32.6 points**, earning entry into the **Hall of Living Legends**. Christian Watson had already supplied **29.7** earlier in the battle.

Forty-two points became thirty. Thirty became twenty. Twenty became ten. Then the entire war came down to fractions.

When the final Monday night numbers settled, the scoreboard read **Winged Victory Formation 118.16. The Beer of Olympus 117.56.** The difference was **0.60 points**.

**Hercules' Palaestra passed into the hands of Winged Victory Formation** by a margin thinner than a sword's edge.

## Apollo's Archers vs. Flight of JarYES
### The Battle Apollo Had to Win Twice
**Apollo's Archers 133.06 • Flight of JarYES 110.26**

Caleb Williams erupted for **37.3 points**. Derrick Henry answered with **34.8**. Together, the two champions produced **72.1 points**, and both entered the **Hall of Living Legends**.

Behind them, Apollo surged to a **91.6–63.3 lead**. It looked decisive. Flight of JarYES refused to accept the judgment.

Jayden Daniels, Omarion Hampton and Trey McBride launched a furious counterattack. When the afternoon battles ended, Flight of JarYES had done the improbable: **Flight of JarYES 110.26. Apollo's Archers 108.26.**

So the Archers had to conquer the battlefield again. Under the Sunday night lights, Isaiah Likely delivered **23.8 points**. The lead returned to Apollo. This time it stayed there.

**133.06–110.26.** And so **Agamemnon's High Hall fell to Apollo's Archers**.

## Uranus Colonizer vs. BeginnersRuck
### The Lead That Vanished at Night
**BeginnersRuck 117.32 • Uranus Colonizer 114.86**

No defeated army controlled its opening battle longer than Uranus Colonizer. Rhamondre Stevenson gave Uranus a **12–0 advantage** on Wednesday. Christian McCaffrey helped extend it to **23.3–0** on Thursday. By Sunday's early games, Uranus still commanded the field: **89.8–56.2.**

Then the road turned. Javonte Williams delivered **21.7 points**, and for the first time in the entire battle, BeginnersRuck moved ahead.

Patrick Mahomes began the pursuit. Point by point, Uranus closed the distance while Travis Kelce and Courtland Sutton fought to preserve BeginnersRuck's advantage. But nearly was all it became.

**BeginnersRuck 117.32. Uranus Colonizer 114.86.** A difference of only **2.46 points**. After spending most of the week behind, **BeginnersRuck captured Moirai Loom**.

And somewhere behind the victorious line stood Jalen Coker, unused and uncalled, sitting on the bench with **29.8 points**, the highest total produced by any benched player in the league that week.

## Thus Began the Odyssey

When the first week ended, six battles had altered the map.

**Atlas' Mountains** belonged to Hercules Unchained. **Hector's Ramparts** belonged to the Peasants of Troy. **Winged Messenger's Rest** belonged to Hades Nutz. **Hercules' Palaestra** belonged to Winged Victory Formation. **Agamemnon's High Hall** belonged to Apollo's Archers. **Moirai Loom** belonged to BeginnersRuck.

Six armies had conquered. Six had fallen.

And six warriors had already carved their names into the **Hall of Living Legends**: **Caleb Williams — 37.3**, **Josh Allen — 35.7**, **Derrick Henry — 34.8**, **Kenneth Walker — 32.6**, **D'Andre Swift — 31.9**, and **Jahmyr Gibbs — 31.1**.

Yet the opening standings revealed almost nothing about the story that would eventually be told. The highest-scoring army had begun 1–0. So had an army that scored only the ninth-most points. The fifth-highest score of the week had produced a loss. One victor had survived by six-tenths. Another had trailed for almost the entire battle. One army had needed to win its war twice. Another had watched a 31-point advantage become a 55-point defeat.

These were not yet trends. They were merely the first pages.

No one standing on the battlefield that week knew which triumphs would become foundations, which defeats would become warnings, or which seemingly insignificant moments would one day look inevitable in hindsight.

They knew only that the first territories had fallen. The first legends had risen. And somewhere among the twelve armies now marching toward the mountain was the one whose banner would eventually fly above them all.

**This was simply where the story began.**
